# ELDORIA-002 (A, B, C)

## 002-A – Private dispatch (English), Richard Stoyner to Oliver Cromwell, 15 Sept 1656
Captain Stoyner (Commonwealth warship) seized a **Spanish churchman** examining sea routes. Key facts:
- He carried folded leaves and the **drawing of a strange compass**: not a compass rose but **several wheels whose devices come from the peoples of the New Indies** (Maya). Given to him by the **Bishopric of Yucatán**.
- He **spoiled a chart by effacing the cardinal mark** that governed its reading (= which way is north), but **copied the mark first**.
- He escaped on the **wreck of a vessel**, then **buried a chest**; can't recall where.
- Chest may hold a writing / declaration / testimony.
- Kept repeating **Juan Ponce de León**; fountain of youth in Florida; Order of Santiago.
- Year 1656 = year the *Maravillas* sank (practice puzzle 2 answer). Probably the wreck he escaped from.

## 002-B – Survivor's memoir (Spanish), headed "✝ J. M. J."
Our transcription (rough) and translation of the key parts:
- Memoir written after the shipwreck, to keep a secret that "must belong to no prince of this world".
- He was entrusted with a **document**; it was **not lost in the waters** as Spain may be told — he took it on purpose.
- "**La carta señala una tierra que el documento nombra «Eldoria»; la brújula designa su tesoro más precioso, que no es de oro. Separadas, estas dos piezas quedan oscuras. Reunidas, llevarán a un hombre resuelto hasta aquello que tantos otros, movidos por sola la codicia, han buscado en vano.**"
  = The chart points to a land the document calls Eldoria; the compass shows its most precious treasure, which is not gold. Apart, the two pieces are dark; together they lead a resolute man to what so many sought in vain out of greed.
- "Pude estragar la carta y copiar el dibujo de la brújula" = I spoiled the chart and copied the compass drawing, which will not leave me.
- The document deserves to be **buried and hidden in a place chosen by me alone**.
- **The place clue:** "Recogido después del naufragio, volví a tomar tierra en algunos parajes, entre ellos **esta ciudad**, donde **tres edificios** detuvieron mi atención…
  **Dos de ellos parecen mirarse el uno al otro: vuelto el uno hacia la rada, el otro hacia la entrada de las aguas, como dos guardianes puestos a la defensa de un mismo umbral. Su parentesco no es fortuito — sé que un mismo ingenio había trazado sus primeros diseños.**
  **El tercero es de otra naturaleza: sus piedras no guardan los cuerpos, sino las almas.**
  **Fue de aquel tercer lugar de donde partí, como conviene a un hombre de fe, a la hora tercera después de mediodía y veinte minutos, muy puntualmente.**"
  = Two buildings face each other, one toward the roadstead, the other toward the entrance of the waters, two guardians of the same threshold, first designed by the same engineer. The third guards souls, not bodies (a church). I left from the third place at **3:20 pm**, very punctually.
- Ends: "Quien leyere estos renglones, muéstrese más digno del secreto de lo que yo lo fui. Laus Deo semper."

## 002-C – Sketch map
Four circles joined by lines: **clock/bell tower** (top) — **shoe/boot-shaped land** (left) — long line — **rabbit** — **butterfly**. A red **X** near the tower–boot line, beside a small tick mark on that line.

## Working ideas (SUSPECT)
- Two forts facing each other across a harbour mouth, designed by the same engineer: **Havana** (El Morro and La Punta, both Bautista Antonelli). Other Antonelli pairs: San Juan, Cartagena, Portobelo. Third building = a church/cathedral.
- 3:20 pm: maybe a direction (clock hand / sun bearing) or a time-based walk from the church.
- Circles on 002-C may be things to find on the Caribbean map.
