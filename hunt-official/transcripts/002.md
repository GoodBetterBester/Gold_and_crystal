# ELDORIA-002 (A, B, C)

## 002-A – Private dispatch (English), Richard Stoyner to Oliver Cromwell, 15 Sept 1656
Captain Stoyner (Commonwealth warship) seized a **Spanish churchman** examining sea routes. Key facts:
- He carried folded leaves and the **drawing of a strange compass**: not a compass rose but **several wheels whose devices come from the peoples of the New Indies** (Maya). Given to him by the **Bishopric of Yucatán**.
- He **spoiled a chart by effacing the cardinal mark** that governed its reading (= which way is north), but **copied the mark first**.
- He escaped on the **wreck of a vessel**, then **buried a chest**; can't recall where.
- Chest may hold a writing / declaration / testimony.
- Kept repeating **Juan Ponce de León**; fountain of youth in Florida; Order of Santiago.
- Year 1656 = year the *Maravillas* sank (practice puzzle 2 answer). Probably the wreck he escaped from.

## 002-B – Survivor's memoir (Spanish), headed "✝ J. M. J."
Our transcription (rough) and translation of the key parts:
- Memoir written after the shipwreck, to keep a secret that "must belong to no prince of this world".
- He was entrusted with a **document**; it was **not lost in the waters** as Spain may be told — he took it on purpose.
- "**La carta señala una tierra que el documento nombra «Eldoria»; la brújula designa su tesoro más precioso, que no es de oro. Separadas, estas dos piezas quedan oscuras. Reunidas, llevarán a un hombre resuelto hasta aquello que tantos otros, movidos por sola la codicia, han buscado en vano.**"
  = The chart points to a land the document calls Eldoria; the compass shows its most precious treasure, which is not gold. Apart, the two pieces are dark; together they lead a resolute man to what so many sought in vain out of greed.
- "Pude estragar la carta y copiar el dibujo de la brújula" = I spoiled the chart and copied the compass drawing, which will not leave me.
- The document deserves to be **buried and hidden in a place chosen by me alone**.
- **The place clue:** "Recogido después del naufragio, volví a tomar tierra en algunos parajes, entre ellos **esta ciudad**, donde **tres edificios** detuvieron mi atención…
  **Dos de ellos parecen mirarse el uno al otro: vuelto el uno hacia la rada, el otro hacia la entrada de las aguas, como dos guardianes puestos a la defensa de un mismo umbral. Su parentesco no es fortuito — sé que un mismo ingenio había trazado sus primeros diseños.**
  **El tercero es de otra naturaleza: sus piedras no guardan los cuerpos, sino las almas.**
  **Fue de aquel tercer lugar de donde partí, como conviene a un hombre de fe, a la hora tercera después de mediodía y veinte minutos, muy puntualmente.**"
  = Two buildings face each other, one toward the roadstead, the other toward the entrance of the waters, two guardians of the same threshold, first designed by the same engineer. The third guards souls, not bodies (a church). I left from the third place at **3:20 pm**, very punctually.
- Ends: "Quien leyere estos renglones, muéstrese más digno del secreto de lo que yo lo fui. Laus Deo semper."

## 002-C – Sketch map
Four circles joined by lines: **clock/bell tower** (top) — **shoe/boot-shaped land** (left) — long line — **rabbit** — **butterfly**. A red **X** near the tower–boot line, beside a small tick mark on that line.

## Working ideas (SUSPECT)
- Two forts facing each other across a harbour mouth, designed by the same engineer: **Havana** (El Morro and La Punta, both Bautista Antonelli). Other Antonelli pairs: San Juan, Cartagena, Portobelo. Third building = a church/cathedral.
- 3:20 pm: maybe a direction (clock hand / sun bearing) or a time-based walk from the church.
- Circles on 002-C may be things to find on the Caribbean map.

So the "boot" circle is probably the shark's fin, and 003 is the close-up of the X on 002-C.

## Correction (13 Nov, Garath)
- The second circle is **not a boot and not the 003 shark fin**. At full resolution it is an open outline: the **pointed end of an island or cape**, with a second inner line along one side (a thin strip of land with water behind it). The 003 fin is a rounded hook and does not match line for line. I overstated that.
- Best candidate (SUSPECT): the **north tip of North Bimini**, turned about 45 degrees clockwise. Thin beach strip with a bump along one side, sharp point, long edge on the other side. Compare `images/002c-cape-vs-north-bimini.png`.
- Why Bimini fits the story: 002-A names Ponce de Leon and the fountain of youth (Bimini legend), and 003 shows a shark (Shark Mound is on Bimini).
- Against: the city with two forts and a church (002-B) is not Bimini, and the tower, rabbit and butterfly are still unplaced.
- Checked and looked worse: Havana, San Juan and Cartagena harbour points, Cuba's west and east tips.

## Hidden-message check on 002-A and 002-B (13 Nov, second thread)
Both pages re-read from the full-resolution images.

**Checked, nothing found (KNOW):**
- First letters of paragraphs and of printed lines, in both documents: no words.
- Capitals: only ordinary names (Providencia, Dios, España, Corona, Eldoria, Creador; New Indies, Yucatan, Florida, Santiago).
- 002-A faded letters: the fading is blotchy paper wear that crosses several letters at a time, not single chosen letters.
- 002-B black versus red-brown ink: the black sits in a band down the left margin and one patch at the right. It follows the stain, not chosen words.
- 002-B odd gaps inside words (despue s, conservacio n, pri ncipe): the font draws every accented letter that way. Not a signal.
- Paper background of 002-B with the writing filtered out, and the dark page borders brightened: no hidden drawing or text.
- The tower in 002-C has no clock hands. The round opening has bars in it.

**Transcription fixes (KNOW):**
- 002-B says "parecen **medirse** el uno al otro" (size each other up), not "mirarse".
- 002-A is signed Richard **Stoyner**. The historical captain who took the Spanish treasure ships in September 1656 was Richard Stayner. Could be a deliberate change or just the designer's spelling.
- The number 20 appears twice: "veinte minutos" in 002-B and "x20" on the 003 scale bar.

**New reading of the four circles (SUSPECT):** they may be the 002-B place paragraph in pictures, not four places on the sea chart.
- Tower = a *garita*, the domed sentry box with a ball on top found on Spanish forts (El Morro in Havana and San Juan). The "legs" are its stone bracket.
- Pointed land = "la punta", so Castillo de **la Punta**, the second guardian.
- Butterfly = the soul (Greek *psyche* means both), so the third building whose stones "guard souls".
- Rabbit = the punctual departure at 3:20 (the rabbit with the watch), or the Maya moon rabbit.
- Read backwards the chain is the walk: church, departure, La Punta, then toward El Morro, with the X off the line at the tick, about halfway. In Havana that spot is in the harbour mouth, which fits the shark of 003.
- Not yet explained: what 3:20 measures, and the x20 bar.

## First circle as a bell, and a wreck-site test (13 Nov, Garath's idea)
- Garath: the first circle may be a **bell**, not a tower. It has a ring on top and widens downward. A porthole and hanging weights would make it a diving bell (SUSPECT).
- Sketch geometry, page top taken as north: bell to cape 222 degrees, cape to rabbit 115 degrees and 1.9 times as long, rabbit to butterfly 194 degrees and 0.48 times as long. X lies 207 degrees from the bell at 0.52 of the bell to cape length.
- Test: bell = Maravillas wreck (about 27.2 N, 78.9 W, only known to roughly 30 km), cape = each candidate, sketch scaled and turned to fit.
  - North Bimini tip: needs a 29 degree turn; rabbit and butterfly land in open ocean east of Abaco.
  - Cape Florida: fits north-up within 6 degrees; rabbit lands 40 km off Cape Eleuthera, butterfly in Exuma Sound.
  - Cape Canaveral, West End, Hole in the Wall: nothing recognisable.
- Result: **inconclusive**. No placement puts the rabbit and butterfly on a recognisable place. The sketch may not be to scale.
